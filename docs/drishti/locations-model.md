# CityKart Drishti — locations, recorders and assets (P2-5a, P2-5b)

Status: database layer built in P2-5a (migrations `20261021000018`…`20261021000021`, review fix round 1 in `20261021000022`; rollbacks in `supabase/rollback/`). **P2-5b** put every application and worker code path on the recorder model (section 11) and removed the transitional seam with the contract migration `20261021000024` (section 7; `20261021000023` adds what the code paths need). The administration and import screens are P2-5c. Decisions: D-4, D-5 (schema), D5-1…D5-12 of `docs/drishti/p2-5-impact-map.md`, ruling C-1 (CONFIRMED), ruling R-1 and its amendment, the owner addition on switching monitoring off (2026-10-22). Access rules: `docs/drishti/access-model.md`.

## 1. The tree

```
Organization ─ Region ─ Cluster ─ Location ─ NVR (recorder) ─ Camera
                                     └──── IT assets (switch, AP, SD-WAN edge, UPS, printer, desktop, …)
```

- **Strict tree** (ruling C-1): a cluster is in at most one region, a location in at most one cluster, a camera on at most one NVR at a time.
- **Region and cluster are optional, permanently** (owner amendment to R-1): `clusters.region_id` and `locations.cluster_id` are nullable; no "Unassigned" rows are made and nothing warns. The UI shows "No cluster". Organisation, location, recorder, camera and asset scopes work for such a location; region and cluster scopes simply do not reach it.
- **Who sees region and cluster names** (impact map 1.9, review M15): the organisation; a grant at the node; a region grant shows its clusters; a cluster grant shows its region; a location grant shows its cluster and region. Recorder, camera and asset grants show no region or cluster (their location row stays visible), so a person with one camera does not learn how the company is organised.
- **Code and name.** `code` is the stable short code (upper case, e.g. `JCL`; unique among the organisation's non-archived rows); `name` is a freely editable display name. Renaming keeps the id, grants, groups and history. `zone` and `state` stay plain optional attributes of a location; `inventory_propose_hierarchy(org)` proposes cluster / region codes from them for an administrator to confirm (nothing is changed automatically).
- **Location kinds:** `store`, `head_office`, `dc`, `warehouse`, `other`. A head office or DC needs no NVR. Optional `parent_location_id` (never a cycle: SQLSTATE `DR008`); `uplink_asset_id` (the SD-WAN edge, D-5) is a same-organisation foreign key (`locations_uplink_fk`, review M14). Because locations and assets now have two relationships, an embed between them names the one it means (`locations!assets_location_fk(...)`); the app does.

## 2. Renames (ids unchanged)

`stores` → `locations`, `devices` → `assets`, `cameras.store_id` / `devices.store_id` / `profiles.store_id` → `location_id`. Done in place, so every id — and so every grant, camera group item, check_state / outage row and monitor gap — keeps pointing at the same row. No compatibility views (the impact map does not recommend them; every caller was updated). The enum type keeps its name `device_type`; P2-5a adds `sdwan_edge`, `switch`, `access_point`, `router`, `ups`, `printer`, `desktop`, `laptop` in its own migration (`20261021000018`, enum values must be committed before use). `assets.assigned_profile_id` (the Wi-Fi profile an AP should carry) has no foreign key yet: the Wi-Fi profiles table arrives with P2-6.

## 3. Recorders and channel mappings

- **`recorders`** is a 1:1 extension of an `assets` row of type `nvr` with the **same id** (D5-2): `http_port`, `rtsp_port`, `username`, `secret_id` (Vault, name `recorder:<id>`; never readable by signed-in people), `expected_channels`, `clock_offset_sec` / `clock_offset_source`, `vendor_capabilities`, the monitoring switch (section 6), `migrated_from_location_id` and `created_asset`. The NVR's address is its asset's `ip` (and its endpoints). A location may have several recorders. Its **primary** recorder (`recorder_primary_of(location)`, and `pickPrimaries` in the app) is the one the 5a data migration made for the location (`migrated_from_location_id` = the location), else the first one made; only the legacy single-NVR store form and the legacy CSV ("location + primary recorder + channels") use it. **`recorder_save(org, location, recorder | NULL, host, http_port, rtsp_port, username, expected_channels, name, actor)`** (P2-5b, server only) creates or changes ONE recorder in one transaction; an address already taken in the company network moves it into its location's own address space (D5-8); audited as `recorder_created` / `recorder_updated`.
- **Clock offset (ruling R13 direction, D-17 data):** `clock_offset_sec` is the seconds to take off a time shown to people to get the time that NVR expects. Playback, the day search and fast playback use the recorder's own value when it has one and the global `PLAYBACK_OFFSET_MIN` otherwise; two NVRs of one location may differ.
- **`camera_channel_mappings (camera_id, recorder_id, channel, valid_from, valid_to)`** is the truth for "which NVR channel shows this camera". The two partial uniques of D-4 — one active mapping per camera, one active camera per recorder channel — plus no-overlap exclusions keep the history consistent. `cameras.recorder_id` and `cameras.channel` are a cache of the active row, kept by triggers. A camera may only map to a recorder at its own location (SQLSTATE `DR006`). A camera without an active mapping is not checked, streamed or played back (review M5).
- **Camera identity is independent of the channel**: `UNIQUE (store_id, channel)` is gone. `camera_remap(org, camera, recorder, channel, at, actor)` (or a direct change of `cameras.recorder_id` / `channel`) closes the active mapping and opens a new one; the camera keeps its id, grants, group membership and monitoring history. Two NVRs at one location may both have channel 1. A remap raises the access version of the holders of a recorder grant on the old and the new NVR (their cameras change; review M8) and, onto an NVR that is not monitored, forgets the camera's state (M6).
- **Credentials:** `recorder_store_credential`, `recorder_read_credential` (refuses, SQLSTATE `55000`, while the NVR's monitoring is switched off: the server never even reads that login; review M15), `recorder_delete_credential` (service role), `recorder_credential_status(org, recorder)`. These are the ONLY path: the location-keyed `nvr_*_credential` wrappers of the seam were removed by the contract migration.
- **A camera inserted without a recorder** is mapped automatically only when its location has exactly ONE NVR (`cameras_after_insert`, P2-5b); with two or more it stays unmapped — never probed, never streamed, "Not checked yet" — until it is mapped explicitly. No camera is ever routed through a guessed NVR.

## 4. Addresses and the SD-WAN unknown

- **`address_spaces`**: one `global` space per organisation ("Company network", made by a trigger for every new organisation); `location_nat` spaces for a location whose addresses overlap others — used only by assets (and so endpoints) of that location (SQLSTATE `23514` otherwise; review M14); `sdwan` / `other` reserved.
- **`asset_endpoints (asset, space, host, port, purpose, retired_at)`**: one live address (host + port, a missing port counting as one value) per space. This **replaces the organisation-wide `UNIQUE (org_id, ip, tcp_port)`** (AL-6): the address is an attribute, not the identity. Endpoints are kept in step with `assets.ip` / `tcp_port` (management) and the recorder's RTSP port by triggers. A recorder's endpoints are readable only with the recorder-level IT view (review I-1).
- **External unknown (dependency 1):** whether the SD-WAN gives every location unique routed ranges or overlapping ranges behind NAT/VRF is not known yet. Until the network team answers, everything is in the one global space; the migration and the seam move a recorder into its location's own `location_nat` space only when its address is already taken in the global space (reported as `address_conflict`). When the answer comes, more spaces are modelled — no identity changes.
- **`network_definitions (location, management_cidr, gateway, dns[], management_vlan, vlan_map, discovery_ranges, sdwan_context)`**: validated on write — gateway inside the CIDR, VLAN ids 1–4094 for the staff / guest / device / other keys, every discovery range inside the management network (review M9; "discovery range … is outside the management network …", SQLSTATE `22023`). `network_validate_address(org, asset, host)` answers `ok`, `outside_location_network`, `no_network_defined`, `not_an_address` or `no_location` — a head office CIDR never applies to a store. `network_address_conflicts(org)` lists every endpoint outside its location's network and overlapping networks in one address space. Conflicts are flagged, never silently allowed (the screens of P2-5c show them).

## 5. Dependencies, capabilities and archive

- **`asset_dependencies (child, parent, kind ∈ network / power / recording)`**: the schema for D-5 (evaluation in WP-4.1). A cycle (and a self-dependency) is refused (SQLSTATE `DR005`, checked under an advisory lock so two concurrent inserts cannot close a loop).
- **`device_capabilities`**: the vendor × model × firmware × operation matrix, seeded only with what `docs/drishti/wifi-capability-research.md` sources (65 rows: NETGEAR WAX615/610/620/630, WBE750, WAX214, WAC510; each row has its `source_ref`); nothing is `lab_validated`; everything else is `unverified` by absence. Server only in P2-5a.
- **Archive instead of delete.** Regions, clusters, locations, assets (and so recorders), cameras and mappings have `archived_at`; a hard `DELETE` is refused (SQLSTATE `DR003`) except by `inventory_purge_test_org` for `test-…` and demo organisations. `inventory_archive(org, kind, id, actor)` / `inventory_restore(…)`:
  - archiving closes the active mapping and an archived node grants nothing; an archived location frees its code; the Vault secret is kept (the purge removes it);
  - refused: a recorder with active cameras (`DR004`); the location's legacy NVR while the location still names an NVR address (`DR009`, review M3: otherwise another NVR would inherit its address); a cluster with active locations or a region with active clusters (`DR007`, review M7: archiving would silently take everybody's access);
  - restore brings back the on/off state from before the archive (`archived_was_active` / `archived_was_enabled`, review M15); **restored cameras come back unmapped** — also when their location is restored — and are not checked or streamed until mapped again (`camera_remap`, or the NVR's camera discovery, which maps unmapped cameras by channel; review M5);
  - both raise the access version (the P2-4 revalidation signal) of exactly the people whose access changes: grants at or above the node, organisation-wide grants, and for a location the recorder, camera and asset grants inside it (`inventory_affected_people`; review M8).
- **Deactivation keeps its tested meaning**: a location made inactive, an asset disabled or a camera deactivated still forgets its current state and closes its open outage; history rows stay (the old delete triggers that erased history are gone).

## 6. Switching monitoring off per NVR (owner addition, 2026-10-22)

`recorders.monitoring_enabled` (default **true**), `monitoring_disabled_reason`, `monitoring_disabled_at`, `monitoring_disabled_by`; a reason of at least 3 characters is required while off (table check). An NVR counts as **monitored** only when its monitoring is switched on **and** its asset is enabled (review M4); otherwise **nothing contacts the NVR or its cameras**: the worker makes no NVR or camera probe for it, results for it or its cameras are ignored and no check_state is written; discovery, opening live streams, opening playback and fast playback refuse with "Monitoring is switched off for this NVR." before any outbound request or password read, and the server function that reads its login refuses too. Tests prove zero sockets with a counting TCP server (`tests/integration/worker-run.test.ts`, `discovery.test.ts`, `open-streams.test.ts`, `playback-streams.test.ts`, `vod.test.ts`, `monitor-db.test.ts`). Switching on again resumes everything. Summaries and status views show **"Switched off"** (`location_status.cameras_monitoring_off` / `recorders_monitoring_off`, `recorder_status.monitoring_enabled`, `getStates` status `off`); a camera of an NVR that is not monitored counts as switched off only — never also live, down, stale or unknown (review M6). Only a super administrator, or an administrator holding `admin.inventory` organisation-wide, may change the switch (`recorder_set_monitoring`), audited. The owner authorised all real NVRs for local use (amendment to R-1); the importer of P2-5c sets the flag explicitly per NVR. A location's NVR is not a device: the device screens refuse to change or delete it ("This is the NVR of a store. Change it on the Manage stores page.").

## 7. Transitional seam (ruling D5-7) — REMOVED by the P2-5b contract migration `20261021000024`

In P2-5a the legacy NVR columns stayed on `locations` with a one-way sync trigger to the location's legacy NVR, location-keyed `nvr_*_credential` wrappers, an `('nvr', location)` alias in `monitor_apply_results`, an `'nvr'` branch in the two monitoring policies and `status_reasons`, and DR009 protecting the legacy address. The contract migration removed all of it once no code read it: the columns `nvr_ip`, `rtsp_port`, `http_port`, `nvr_username`, `nvr_secret_id`, `expected_channels` of `locations`; the trigger `locations_seam`, `locations_legacy_seam()`, `recorder_ensure_primary()`, `recorder_seam_of()`; the three wrappers; `'nvr'` in the `check_state` / `outages` target-type CHECKs and everywhere else; DR009; and the one-time migration machinery that reads the legacy columns (`inventory_migrate_org`, `inventory_unmigrate_org`, their `_test` wrappers, `inventory_secret_fingerprint_test`, the snapshot table `inventory_migration_snapshot`). It refuses to run (nothing changes) while a location names an NVR address that no recorder was made from, holds a vault secret of its own, or any `'nvr'` history row exists. Its self-test scans every function body (`inventory_stale_name_scan()`, which now also knows the seam's names and the `'nvr'` kind), every policy and every view. There never was a compatibility view. The guard against an adopted nvr device being probed twice stays (`recorders_after_write` stops its device monitoring; `monitor_apply_results` ignores `'device'` results for recorder assets), and `20261021000023` closed any such device history once more (data fix). The code guard and database checks are in `tests/integration/contract-p2-5b.test.ts`.

## 8. Data migration (`inventory_migrate_org`, report in `inventory_migration_report`)

(History: the migration ran once, in P2-5a; its functions were removed by the P2-5b contract migration because they read the legacy columns. The report table `inventory_migration_report` stays.) Per organisation, idempotent: each location with an NVR gets its recorder (adopting an `nvr` device at the same address and location: `device_merged`, its device history stopped), its Vault secret is **renamed in place** from `nvr:<location>` to `recorder:<id>` (`vault.update_secret` with a new name only — same ciphertext, verified by hash inside the transaction; never decrypted into any output), every camera gets a mapping on its location's NVR and channel, `check_state` / `outages` rows `('nvr', location)` are re-keyed to `('recorder', recorder)` (respecting the one-open-outage index), and the report lists `recorder_created`, `device_merged`, `address_conflict`, `secret_missing`, `secret_dangling`, `camera_unmapped`, `device_unlocated` (an nvr device without a location at the NVR's address: not adopted) and `device_duplicate` (another nvr device of the location at that address: not adopted) plus an `org_summary` (review M13: reported, never adopted silently, never an abort). Before anything changes, `20261021000019` snapshots every person's effective sets with the **old** functions; `20261021000021` verifies against it and aborts on any difference: camera and location sets equal, asset sets equal except the NVR assets the migration made for locations the person already covered whole. `inventory_unmigrate_org` reverses one organisation (database owner only; used by the rollback and, through `inventory_unmigrate_org_test`, by the tests to build legacy-shaped data). The tests also compare every legacy kind of person with **hand-written expectations from the old rules** (independent of the new functions) and the recorder-level IT visibility through each person's own sign-in (`tests/integration/migration-equality-p2-5a.test.ts`).

Local result (2026-10-22, `npx supabase migration up --local` on the owner's dev data after a data-only backup): the CityKart organisation got 1 recorder with its 31 cameras mapped and its secret renamed in place; the second local organisation's store got a recorder with 1 check_state and 1 outage re-keyed and no secret saved (`secret_missing`). Every effective set was unchanged. Fix round 1 (`20261021000022`, same way, new data-only backup first) stopped the 'device' monitoring of every adopted NVR device (none had any locally).

## 10. Applying and rolling back (runbook)

- **Apply only through `npx supabase migration up --local`** (or `supabase db push` for a hosted database): the CLI runs each migration file in one transaction. `20261021000019`…`22` contain no BEGIN / COMMIT of their own; fed statement by statement to a client without a transaction (`psql` without `-1`) they could stop half-way (review M1). `20261021000018` must be its own file (enum values must be committed before use). Take a data-only backup first (`npx supabase db dump --local --data-only -f <file>`); never reset the database.
- **Rollback**, in this order: `supabase/rollback/20261021000024…` (P2-5b contract: puts the seam back — legacy columns rebuilt from each location's recorder made by the 5a migration, else its first recorder, which is then marked as that one; every dropped function verbatim from the database before the contract; the `'nvr'` kind; an empty snapshot table; it never needs to refuse), `…23` (removes `recorder_save` and `inventory_refresh_statistics`, puts back the "map to the primary NVR" camera insert rule and the old `search_path` of the two stop helpers; the device-history data fix stays), `supabase/rollback/20261021000022…` (the previous definitions verbatim from 0019–0021; the data fix of adopted devices and raised access versions stay), `…21` (unmigrate every organisation: recorders of the migration removed, secrets renamed back to `nvr:<location>`, history re-keyed back), `…20` (network definitions, dependencies, capability matrix), `…19` (old tables, functions, policies, views and triggers verbatim from the pre-migration schema; it refuses while recorders, region / cluster / recorder grants, archived rows, locations without an NVR, assets without an address or assets of a type added by `…18` exist), `…18` is a no-op (enum values cannot be dropped). Dry-run of the whole chain in one rolled-back transaction: the schema's functions, tables, views, policies, triggers, keys and indexes equal the pre-migration dump, `privilege_violations()` is empty, and the forward migrations 0019–0022 then apply again with the equality check passing. P2-5b repeated it for 0024 → 0019 and forward 0019 → 0024 on the owner's data: the public schema fingerprint (every function's normalised body, ACL and settings, every constraint, policy, column, trigger and grant) after the round trip equals the one before, and the 0024 rollback alone gives back exactly the pre-contract schema and the legacy column values.
- **After a restore** (location, recorder or camera): restored cameras come back unmapped (5a ruling) — run "Find cameras" for the store (it asks every NVR of the location; each NVR puts the cameras that were on its channels back, and lists anything it cannot place for an administrator to reconcile instead of making a second camera), or map them in the P2-5c mapping editor. Until then they show "Not checked yet" and cannot be opened.
- **After a bulk import or "discover all"**, the code refreshes the planner statistics (`inventory_refresh_statistics()`); run it by hand after any other bulk change (on stale statistics `location_status` took 1.8 s instead of 18 ms on the VA-20 shape).

## 11. The application on the recorder model (P2-5b)

- **Worker** (`lib/monitor/*`): one NVR job per **monitored** recorder (monitoring switched on, asset enabled and not archived, location active) — its own address, video and web ports, login name and password (read once per round through `recorder_read_credential(recorder)`, only when it has cameras and is not known to be down). Its cameras are the ones whose **active** mapping is on it, with the channel of that mapping; a camera without one is never probed ("Not checked yet"). Results are `('recorder', recorder id)` / `('camera', id)`; a recorder known to be down marks ITS cameras down at once with cause `nvr_down` (threshold 1) — cameras of the location's other NVRs are their own jobs and are not touched. The login back-off (R9), the login fingerprint, the serialised lane and the RTSP fan-out are per recorder; R8 (no results on login problems) and R10 (stale after 20 minutes) are unchanged. `RECORDER_CONCURRENCY` (the old `STORE_CONCURRENCY` is still accepted) sets how many NVRs are checked at once (default 10).
- **Streams** (`lib/streams/open.ts`, `playback.ts`, `vod.ts`, `source.ts`): `resolveCameraRoutes` gives each camera the recorder of its active mapping; that recorder's address, ports, login and clock offset are used; a switched-off NVR or an unmapped camera is refused before any login is read or any socket is opened; demo video is decided by the recorder's (loopback) address. Stream names stay `cam_<camera id>_…`.
- **Discovery** (`lib/monitor/discovery.ts`): per recorder. A listed channel goes to the camera mapped to that recorder's channel; else to an unmapped camera that was on it (or any unmapped one when the location has one NVR); else, when another camera of the location looks like it (same reported address or same name), nothing is created and the channel is listed as `possible_move` for reconciliation; else a new camera mapped to that channel. Cameras whose channel the NVR no longer lists are listed as `missing` (never deleted); renamed ones as `renamed`. `discoverStoreCameras` asks every NVR of a location in turn; `discoverAllRecorders` every NVR of the organisation, at most 4 (up to 8) at a time with progress, switched-off ones skipped unasked.
- **Inventory** (`lib/inventory/stores.ts`, `lib/import/*`): the store form and the legacy CSV (`name, city, zone, ip, port, http_port, user, password, channels`) are "location + primary recorder + channels": the location row, its primary recorder through `recorder_save`, the password through `recorder_store_credential`, and cameras on channels 1..N of THAT recorder (a channel an unmapped camera was on gets that camera back). A location's other NVRs are never touched. Archive and restore: `lib/inventory/archive.ts`.
- **Queries and pages**: `getStates(…, 'recorder', ids)` (the `'nvr'` kind is gone); the dashboard lists every NVR that is down (with its location) and counts NVRs not checked lately; a camera outage is grouped only under the open outage of its OWN recorder; the location page shows each NVR the person has the IT view of, with its cameras grouped under it, and one plain camera list for everybody else.
- **Demo**: `npm run fake:nvr` serves two pretend NVRs (ports 8081 and 8083, video ports 8554 and 8556); `npm run demo:seed` makes DEMO3 a location with TWO NVRs (the second pretend NVR, up, and one nobody answers, down — only its cameras show the recorder as the cause), DEMO2 a single NVR that is down, DEMO1 a single NVR that answers.

### Performance at the VA-20 size (P2-5b re-measure)

390 recorders, 8,100 mapped cameras, ~8,500 check_state rows, local stack, after `inventory_refresh_statistics()`: the worker loads all NVR checks in 6 ms and all 390 recorder jobs with their cameras (incl. 390 login reads) in 207 ms; one full camera round's results go to `monitor_apply_results` in 41 calls of 200 (median 68 ms, max 71 ms, 2.7 s in all), the NVR round in 65 ms. Page reads as the person (medians, ms): count cameras 7–17, list locations 4–5, one camera check 3, batch of 1000 5–9, location status 7–14 (full row 11–20), summary 3–4. On stale statistics the organisation-wide full `location_status` row took 0.8–1.8 s (a nested loop of cameras × check_state), hence the refresh after bulk changes.
