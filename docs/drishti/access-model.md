## The rules

1. **No role gives access to data, and no role alone allows an operation.** `user_role` (`user`, `operator`, `manager`, `admin`, `platform_owner`) is administrative authority only: who may manage people (`canManageRole`), who may use the admin pages (always together with an `admin.*` capability) and who may edit camera groups (`hasGroupEditingAuthority`, always together with `cctv.live`). What a person may see or do comes only from grants. The human role `agent` was renamed `operator` (AC-15).
2. **A grant is one capability at one scope**, for one person, with an optional validity window: `access_grants (capability, scope_kind, scope_id, valid_from, valid_until, revoked_at, source, needs_review, …)`.
3. **Scopes** (seven kinds since P2-5a, ruling C-1). `organization` = everything of the organisation. `region` = every location of every cluster in the region; `cluster` = every location in the cluster; `location` = every camera / asset in that location. These three expand **when access is evaluated**, so a location, camera or asset added later — or a location moved into the cluster — is covered at once, and one moved out stops being covered at once. `recorder` = the cameras whose active channel mapping is on that NVR (CCTV), or the NVR's own asset and the assets attached to it (IT); never a whole location. `camera` = exactly that camera. `asset` = exactly that asset. Archived regions, clusters, locations, recorders, cameras and assets grant nothing, and an archived region or cluster cuts the path below it. A location need not belong to a cluster nor a cluster to a region (owner amendment to R-1): such a location is reached by organisation, location, recorder, camera and asset scopes only.
4. **Union, never narrowing.** A person's access for a capability is the union of their live grants. There is no deny and no rule where a narrower grant hides a wider one: adding camera grants to a location grant never takes cameras away (AC-6). To narrow, revoke the location grant and grant cameras.
5. **A grant counts** only while the person is active, has chosen their password, the grant is not revoked, `valid_from <= now() < valid_until` (no end = open), and — while `mfa_enforced()` is switched on for the person's organisation (P3-1: `security_settings.mfa_enforced`, database owner only; off on the local stack) — the session is `aal2` for capabilities with `requires_mfa`. The app's own gate, fresh sign-in and the factor reset: `docs/drishti/ARCHITECTURE.md` section 5.9.
6. **History is append-only.** Grants are never updated or deleted by the app or the service role. Revoking sets `revoked_at/by/reason`; deleting the camera, location or asset revokes the grant and keeps the row; deleting a person keeps their grants as history (only deleting the organisation removes them). A person's `profiles.access_version` goes up with every change.
7. **Granting is monotonic.** Granting what a live grant already covers (same capability and scope, a window inside its window) changes nothing. Otherwise the new window is joined with the live grants of the same capability and scope that are **in effect now or overlap / touch it** (P2-3 review M-2, `access_merge_plan` in `20261021000017`): they are replaced by one grant over all of them (earliest start, latest end, or no end if any has none). A live grant that has **ended** (or starts later) and does not touch the new window is history and stays exactly as it is; a separate new grant is added next to it. So a person may have several live (not revoked) grants of the same right and scope, never with overlapping windows (exclusion constraint `access_grants_live_no_overlap`). A grant can therefore never shorten, postpone or narrow access; only a revocation takes access away. The preview applies the same rule.
8. **The home location** (`profiles.location_id`, `store_id` before P2-5a) is information only and grants nothing (AC-7).
9. **CCTV never shows IT.** Assets, their status and NVR (recorder) status, addresses and logins need `it.inventory.view` or `it.monitoring.view` (AC-4). CCTV capabilities show camera rows, camera status (including "Switched off" when monitoring of the camera's NVR is off) and the plain location row; a CCTV grant at recorder scope still shows cameras only, never the recorder row.

## Functions

Signed-in callers (about themselves only): `has_capability(cap)`, `has_capability_for(cap, 'camera'|'location'|'asset'|'recorder'|'cluster'|'region', id)` (a location, cluster or region counts only when covered whole, through its ancestry), `capability_camera_ids(cap)`, `capability_location_ids(cap)` (locations covered whole; `capability_store_ids` before P2-5a), `capability_asset_ids(cap)` (`capability_device_ids`), `any_cctv_camera_ids()`, `cameras_with_capability(cap, ids[])`, `any_grant_location_ids()` (`any_grant_store_ids`), `any_grant_cluster_ids()`, `any_grant_region_ids()`, `it_view_location_ids()` (`it_view_store_ids`), `it_view_asset_ids()` (`it_view_device_ids`), `it_view_recorder_ids()`, `cctv_visible_group_ids()`, `cameras_monitoring_off(ids)` (P2-5a: which of these cameras sit on a switched-off NVR), `mfa_enforced()`, and (P2-2, `20261021000013`, `authenticated` only) `my_access_summary()`: one row per capability that counts now, with `organization_wide` and the granted `location_ids`, `camera_ids`, `asset_ids`, and since P2-5a `region_ids`, `cluster_ids`, `recorder_ids` (the grants' scopes) plus `covered_location_ids` (locations covered whole after expanding regions and clusters) and `recorder_camera_ids` (cameras on granted recorders), evaluated by `access_grants_of` so status, validity, revocation and MFA rules stay in one place; and (fix round 1, `20261021000014`, `authenticated` only) `status_reasons(type, ids)` (P2-5a: also `recorder`).

Server only (service role; the Server Action checks the administrator first and passes its organisation; the acting person `actor` must be an active person of that organisation): `grant_access(org, user, grants, actor, reason, expected_version)`, `revoke_grant(org, user, grant_ids, …)`, `replace_user_grants(org, user, capabilities[], scope_kinds[], grants, …)` (makes the live grants for those capabilities and scope kinds exactly the list; others untouched; a wanted description without dates matches any live grant of that scope and leaves it as it is, so nothing is re-dated), `preview_access_change(org, user, changes, at, sample)` (per capability: added/removed counts and samples, writes nothing; revocations first, then grants joined as above), `access_expiring(org, within)`, `function_privileges()`.

P2-3 fix round 1 (`20261021000017`, server only): `change_person_role`, `set_person_active`, `access_review_self_confirm`, `access_teardown_test_org_people` (test/demo clean-up only); internal: `access_merge_plan`, `access_owner_only`, `access_check_people_admin`, `profiles_keep_super_admin`. `grant_platform_owner_all` gained an optional acting person (`p_actor`). The test helper `access_catalogue_probe_test` stays in the schema (the suite reaches the database only through PostgREST); it works only on `test-…` organisations and always rolls itself back.

P2-3 (server only, `20261021000015` / `20261021000016`): `apply_access_change(org, user, changes, actor, expected_version)` (revocations with reasons and grants in one transaction; actor required), `access_review_keep(org, actor, grant_ids, note)`, `access_grant_summary(org)` (per person and capability: organisation-wide, number of locations / cameras / assets, next expiry; in effect now), `grant_platform_owner_all(org, user)`, and the test helper `access_catalogue_probe_test(org)` (test organisations only; inserts a probe capability in a block that is always rolled back). Internal, callable by nobody: `access_check_manager`, `access_check_grantor` and the two trigger functions.

Reporting and diagnostics only — never for authorisation: `report_effective_camera_ids/asset_ids(user, cap, at, ignore_status)` (`report_effective_device_ids` before P2-5a) (treat every session as MFA-verified), `access_session_capabilities(user, claims, mfa_enforced)` (the capabilities that would count for given JWT claims), `access_legacy_*` (the old rules). The migration `access_migrate_legacy(run, users, force)` is for the database owner only and skips anybody who already has a report row unless `force`; tests use `access_migrate_legacy_test`, which refuses people outside organisations made by the test suite.

A grant description is `{capability, scope_kind, scope_id?, valid_from?, valid_until?}`. A change for the preview is that plus `op: 'grant'`, or `{op: 'revoke', grant_id}` (the person's own live grant). A stale `expected_version` fails with SQLSTATE `40001` (lost-update guard).

The migration proves itself and aborts if any check fails: the legacy evaluator equals the real old functions for every person who can sign in; afterwards every person's live and playback camera sets equal their old camera set; no export outside administrators; no high-risk IT capability. One `access_migration_report` row per person holds the old model, the resulting grants and a note. The old tables `user_store_access`, `user_zone_access`, `user_camera_access` are retired: no policy, no access for signed-in people, read by no access rule; they stay one release with the `access_legacy_*` functions (migration report, VA-18 test), then go.

## Enforcement in the app (P2-2)

Two kinds of question, answered differently on purpose:

1. **"What may this person do at all, and roughly where?"** — the request's summary. `getCurrentProfile()` (`lib/auth/profile.ts`, cached per request with React `cache`) reads `my_access_summary()` once through the person's own client and exposes `profile.capabilities` (`lib/auth/capabilities.ts`: per capability `{ organization, locations, cameras, assets }`, and `failed` when it could not be read; a failure holds nothing — fail closed). It decides what to **show**: navigation (`lib/nav.ts`), page gates (`requireCapability(cap | caps)` sends home), the admin gate (`requireAdministration(admin.*)`, `mayAdminister`), buttons, links, wall tiles and index lists (`coversCamera`, `coversLocation`, `lib/queries/access.ts`). Data is still read through the person's own client (row security), so a wrong display decision can never reveal data.
2. **"May this person do THIS to THAT camera / location / asset?"** — always the database's scope-specific functions as the person, through `lib/auth/resource.ts`: `camerasFor` (`cameras_with_capability`), `cameraFor` / `locationFor` / `assetFor` (`has_capability_for`), `coversSomeLocation` (`capability_location_ids`). Never the any-scope `has_capability(cap)` for a resource, and never the `report_*` / `access_session_*` helpers (they skip MFA; tooling only). Only after an allowed answer is anything read with the service role (NVR address, login, password; camera addresses), and then scoped to the person's organisation. A refused camera looks exactly like a missing one.

**Administrative rule (one, consistent).** Every `/admin` page and admin action needs an administrative role (`admin` / `platform_owner`) **and** its `admin.*` capability for the whole organisation: people → `admin.users`; access editor → `admin.access`; stores, devices, import, the monitoring page and camera discovery → `admin.inventory`. The services behind them keep their role check as authority (`isAdminRole`, `canManageRole`). Existing administrators hold these by migration (`migrated:admin`); a new administrator needs them granted (the dev-admin script does; the P2-3 screen will).

**Camera status for CCTV-only viewers (ruling on review note 4).** Camera status is camera availability, so everybody who may see a camera sees whether it is live or offline. The checker's own reason names recorder internals, so it is shown only with the IT view of the camera's location; everybody else sees "Recorder not reachable." when the camera's NVR is down (cause `nvr_down`) and "The camera is not responding." otherwise (`cameraReason` in `lib/queries/status.ts`). NVR status, NVR login hints, NVR addresses, devices and camera addresses need the IT view (AC-4). The home page has a CCTV section and an IT section, each shown only with its capability, and "Nothing is assigned to your account yet. Ask an administrator for access." when there is nothing to monitor.

**Status reasons (fix round 1, I-1, migration `20261021000014`).** `check_state.error` and `check_state.method` are not selectable by signed-in people (column privileges: every other column is). The reason a target is offline comes from `status_reasons(type, ids)` (signed-in callers, about what they may see): a camera's reason is the checker's text only with the IT view of its location, otherwise "Recorder not reachable." (method `nvr_down`) or "The camera is not responding."; device and NVR reasons need the IT view of the device or store. `getStates` reads it for offline targets. Checked for the same class of leak: `outages.cause` can only be `nvr_down` (constraint; camera availability), `store_status` (security invoker) uses only status and time columns, devices / endpoint views already need the IT view, `stores` / `cameras` have no address columns for signed-in people.

**Recorder trouble during playback (fix I-2; P2-5b per recorder).** A missing or unreadable NVR login and every NVR answer problem (refused login, no answer, odd answer, slow download) during the calendar, the day search, 1x playback, fast playback and its probe is "The recordings of this camera cannot be reached right now." (fast playback: 502) unless the person has `it.inventory.view` or `it.monitoring.view` for the camera's OWN recorder — the recorder of its active mapping (`recorderDetailsAllowed`, `has_capability_for(…, 'recorder', id)`: organisation, region, cluster, location or recorder grant, the same rule as `it_view_recorder_ids`, review I-1); the IT view gets the specific words (and fast playback's 409 for a missing login). The kind of trouble is logged on the server only. A camera of a switched-off NVR is refused with "Monitoring is switched off for this NVR.", a camera without an active mapping with "This camera is not linked to an NVR yet." (fast playback: 409 for both), before any login is read or any NVR is asked.

**Unreadable summary (fix M-4).** When `my_access_summary` cannot be read, live opening, the playback actions and fast playback answer the plain "Could not check your access. Please try again." (fast playback: 503, audited as `cctv_export_denied` with reason `access_check_failed`), never "not available for your account"; pages show "Your access could not be read just now".

**Fast playback (ruling M-3).** `/api/vod` and its probe need `cctv.export` AND `cctv.playback` for the camera: it is a recording. Export alone gets the same refusal as no right at all (403 without either capability, 404 "Camera not found." when the camera is not covered by both).

**Request counts.** Each capability has its own per-person count (`lib/streams/request-limit.ts`): live (open + refresh, 30/min), playback open (30/min), recording search (60/min), export (30/min). This replaces ruling R17's single shared count. **Order (M-7):** the count is taken before the per-camera scope check, deliberately: a flood of requests for cameras one may not use costs at most the count, never one database check per request beyond it; it reveals nothing (the count is per person, not per camera). The general limiter (`lib/rate-limit.ts`, check-now 3/min, sign-in) is one table per process (`processSingleton`).

**Audit (best effort, `lib/auth/access-audit.ts`; durable audit is P5).** Actor, capability, camera or location ids, count; never a token, address, URL or password: `cctv_live_opened`, `cctv_playback_searched`, `cctv_playback_opened`, `cctv_export_started` / `cctv_export_ended` / `cctv_export_denied`, `check_requested`, `cameras_discovered`, `group_*`.

## Locations, regions, clusters and recorders (P2-5a)

Ruling C-1 (CONFIRMED) and the owner amendment to R-1; migrations `20261021000019` (schema, access functions, seam) and `20261021000021` (data migration). The data model is in `docs/drishti/locations-model.md`.

- **One place for the scope rules.** `access_location_expand(org, regions, clusters, locations)` returns the locations covered whole; `access_camera_set`, `access_asset_set` and `access_location_set` (now with region, cluster and recorder lists) build every effective set from it; `access_ancestry(org, kind, id)` gives a node's current path upwards (an archived node cuts the path) and `access_scope_covers` answers "does this scope cover that target" for the grantor rule, `has_capability_for` and `access_check_spec`. Every policy, preview, report, summary and check uses these — nothing in the app recomputes the hierarchy (`coversCamera` / `coversLocation` use the summary's `covered_location_ids` and `recorder_camera_ids`).
- **Dynamic, immediate.** Moving a location to another cluster (`inventory_move_location`) or a cluster to another region (`inventory_move_cluster`) changes access at once, bumps the `access_version` of the holders of a cluster or region grant at the old or new parent (so open video sessions are revalidated, P2-4) and writes `hierarchy_moved` to `admin_audit_log` with the affected people. `preview_hierarchy_change(org, kind, id, target)` shows who would gain or lose what before the move. Archiving a location, recorder, camera or asset takes its access away at once, restoring gives it back, and both bump the access version of exactly the people whose access changes (grants at or above the node, organisation-wide grants, and for a location the recorder, camera and asset grants inside it; `inventory_affected_people`); a camera remap bumps the holders of a recorder grant on the old and new NVR (review M8). A cluster or region can be archived only when nothing active is left in it (`DR007`, review M7).
- **Renaming keeps everything.** A location's `code` is its stable short code (e.g. JCL); its `name` is freely editable — the id, grants, groups and history stay (test "renaming a location…" in `tests/integration/region-cluster-access.test.ts`).
- **Recorder scope.** A CCTV grant on an NVR covers the cameras whose **active** mapping is on it (a camera remapped to another NVR leaves the grant at once); an IT grant on it covers the NVR's asset, assets attached to it, and the recorder-level IT view of that NVR (its login name, its cameras' addresses and mappings, its states and reasons). A recorder grant never makes a location whole, so it never shows the location's other cameras, its assets or its other NVRs. **Asset scope on the NVR's asset is narrower than recorder scope** (review I-1): it shows that asset row only (an NVR is never monitored as a device, so there is no device status either).
- **Grantor rule** (rule 4 above) uses the target's ancestry: a cluster administrator may give region-wide access only if they hold the region.
- **Monitoring switched off (owner addition).** `recorders.monitoring_enabled` (default on). When off, nothing contacts that NVR or its cameras (worker, discovery, live, playback, fast playback; tests prove zero sockets). Changing it: `recorder_set_monitoring(org, recorder, enabled, reason, actor)` — the actor must be a super administrator, or an administrator holding `admin.inventory` organisation-wide; a reason of at least 3 characters; audited as `recorder_monitoring_disabled` / `recorder_monitoring_enabled`. CCTV viewers see the cameras as "Switched off"; the IT view sees the NVR as "Switched off".
- **Unchanged:** the union rule, append-only grants, the monotonic merge, every P2-1..P2-3 fix, ruling E-1 (export only for administrators). The migration proved it: every person's effective camera and location sets were equal before and after (`tests/integration/migration-equality-p2-5a.test.ts`, run on the 5a tree; retired by P2-5b because the legacy shape it rebuilt no longer exists — the evidence stays in the P2-5a report and the Evidence log); asset sets differ only by the NVR assets the migration made for locations the person already covered whole.

### P2-5b: the application on the recorder model

Stream names stay `cam_<camera id>_…` / `pb_<camera id>_…`: the camera, not the channel, is the identity, so a remapped camera keeps its name and its viewers.

## Revoking open video (P2-4)

Decision D-3, directive AC-14 / CC-10, migration `20261021000029_p24_media_sessions.sql` (rollback in `supabase/rollback/`), code in `lib/streams/registry.ts`, `lib/streams/gateway.ts`, `lib/streams/session-watch.ts`, `lib/streams/{open,playback,vod}.ts`.

**The registry.** Every video connection is one row of `media_sessions` (service role only; signed-in people cannot read it): organisation, person, kind (`live`, `playback`, `vod`, `export_download`, `remote_view`, `remote_control`), camera (or asset), the capability of the kind (`cctv.live`, `cctv.playback`, `cctv.export`; `vod` also needs `cctv.playback`, P2-2 M-3), the go2rtc stream name, the connect window (`connect_by`, 90 s), `expires_at` (window + 30 minutes, the gateway's longest socket; downloads: the longest transfer), `opened_at`, `last_validated_at`, `revoked_at` / `revoke_reason`, `closed_at`.

**One rule, the same SQL.** Whether a session may go on is `media_session_problem`: closed, revoked, expired, the person not active or with a password to choose (`deactivated`, `password_reset`), the camera not streamable (`not_available`: camera switched off or archived, its location off or archived, no active mapping, its NVR switched off or archived — an open stream must not keep contacting a switched-off NVR), or the capability no longer covering the camera (`access_changed`). The capability is evaluated by `has_capability_for_user(user, cap, kind, id)`, which is `has_capability_for` with the caller replaced by a parameter — so organisation, region, cluster, location, recorder and camera scopes expand when evaluated, through the camera's ACTIVE mapping, exactly as everywhere else. The migration refuses to apply if the two bodies ever differ; `tests/integration/media-sessions.test.ts` compares their answers for 12 people × 3 capabilities × 14 targets.

**The flow.**
1. `openStreams` / `openPlayback` call `media_session_create` (which checks the capability for the person once more and leaves out what it would refuse) and sign one token per stream with its `sid` (`lib/streams/token.ts`: tokens without `sid` are rejected; 90 s window). Fast playback (`/api/vod`) makes a `vod` session per download and opens it at once. No session can be made → nothing is opened and no NVR password is read.
2. The gateway verifies the token, then `media_session_open(sid, u, c, kind, stream)`: the session must be that person's, for that camera, kind and stream (a forged sid of somebody else is refused and their session left untouched), not opened before (a copied token cannot open a second socket), within its window and still allowed. Refused → close 1008 before any upstream connection; registry unreachable → 1013 "Cannot verify access".
3. Every 15 s (`DEFAULT_VALIDATE_EVERY_MS`) one batched `media_sessions_still_valid(sids)` for all open sockets; invalid ones close with **4403 "Access changed"** and are marked revoked; valid ones are stamped. A socket whose last good check is 45 s old (`DEFAULT_FAIL_CLOSED_AFTER_MS`) is closed when the registry cannot be asked (fail closed). Fast playback runs the same check inside its response (`watchSession`, 15 s / 45 s) and aborts the NVR transfer and ffmpeg.
4. **Access changes mark sessions at once.** A deferred constraint trigger on `profiles` (`profiles_media_sessions`) runs at commit of every change of a person's `access_version`, status, password flag, role or organisation: deactivation, password reset and role change revoke all of their sessions; anything else (grant added or removed, hierarchy move, camera remap, merge, archive — all of which raise the access version of exactly the affected people) re-checks them and revokes exactly those that lost access. It never fails the change itself. The people screen (`lib/admin/users.ts`) also calls `media_sessions_revoke_user` on deactivation, password reset and role change, and the access editor (`lib/admin/access.ts`) `media_sessions_revalidate` after removals. Grant expiry needs no event: the periodic check re-evaluates `valid_until`.
5. `media_session_close` when the socket or download ends (idempotent). `media_sessions_prune(keep, unopened_keep, org)` deletes finished sessions after 30 days and never-opened ones after a day (the live wall renews its tokens every minute; the worker calls it in P5).

**Guarantees, measured** (`tests/integration/media-revocation.test.ts`: real local database, real gateway with its REST registry client, fake go2rtc; P2-4 report): with the production interval a removed grant closed the socket after 14.8 s; with a 2 s test interval every kind of change closed it in 0.6–2.0 s (grant removed or ended, deactivation, password reset, role change, editor removal, location moved to another cluster, camera moved to another NVR, recorder grant removed, camera and location archived); other people's sockets stayed open. Registry stopped answering: every socket closed after 45.9 s. `media_sessions_still_valid` for 300 open sessions: about 75–90 ms per call, one call per interval. Worst case by design: 15 s + 5 s call = 20 s normally, 45 s + 5 s = 50 s with the database unreachable — inside 60 s.

**Not covered (limits).**
- Frames already delivered to the browser (its playback buffer, a few seconds) keep showing until the player drains them; a screenshot or a screen recording made before the cut cannot be taken back.
- go2rtc keeps a stream registered (its producer) after the last socket closes until it is pruned when idle; revocation closes the sockets and so the consumers, pruning removes idle producers (`lib/streams/go2rtc.ts`). An idle producer serves nobody: every new socket needs a new session.
- A file already downloaded by fast playback stays with the person; only the running transfer is cut.
- The page's own router cache can show already-rendered pages for up to 30 s (`staleTimes.dynamic`); it holds no video.
- MFA: `aal2` is stored per session. P3-1: fast playback (`vod`, which needs `cctv.export`) passes the session's verified level (`my_session_status`) when it makes its session, so while the switch is on it counts only for aal2; live and playback need no MFA capability.
- P2-6 (D-14): the gateway uses its own database role `video_gateway` (key `SUPABASE_VIDEO_GATEWAY_KEY`), which may call `media_session_open`, `media_sessions_still_valid` and `media_session_close` and nothing else; the web app makes sessions through its video path's role `media_service` and ends them (revocation, revalidation) through its own role `app_web` (`docs/drishti/adr-service-roles.md`). In development a missing key falls back to the service key with a warning. Remote sessions (`remote_view`, `remote_control`) have their kinds and checks but are made in P7.

## Who may grant what (P2-3, brief item 6; migration `20261021000015`)

Checked three times with the same rules: by the editor before anything is sent (to explain), by the Server Action (`lib/admin/access-rules.ts` `manageRefusal` / `grantRefusal`, called from `lib/admin/access.ts`), and by the database for every grant and revocation made by a person (`access_check_manager`, `access_check_grantor`, called from `access_put_grant`, `grant_access`, `revoke_grant`, `replace_user_grants`, `apply_access_change`, `access_review_keep`). Changes without an acting person (the migration, the dev-admin script, the demo seed, test fixtures) are the server's own tooling and are not reachable from any screen.

1. The acting person is active, has chosen a password, has the `admin` or `platform_owner` role and holds `admin.access` for the whole organisation now.
2. **Nobody changes their own access** — no grant, no removal, no review confirmation of their own grants — not even a super administrator. Another administrator does it.
3. A plain administrator manages only viewers, operators and managers (as `canManageRole`). Only a super administrator creates, promotes, demotes, deactivates, resets or changes the access of another super administrator; an administrator can neither revoke nor narrow a super administrator's grants.
4. **A plain administrator gives only what they hold themselves**, in effect now, at an equal or wider scope (organisation > region > cluster > location > recorder > camera / asset: organisation covers everything; a region its clusters, their locations and everything in them; a cluster its locations; a location itself and its recorders, cameras and assets; a recorder its mapped cameras, its own asset and assets attached to it; a camera or asset only itself — through the target's current ancestry, `access_scope_covers`, P2-5a) and **for no longer** than they hold it (an open-ended grant needs an open-ended holding; a dated one a holding that lasts at least as long).
5. `it.os.deploy`, `it.credentials.admin`, `it.remote.control`, `it.script.run`, `it.software.manage`, `it.config.deploy`, `it.patch.manage` and (review ruling M-9, symmetric with `it.credentials.admin`) `wifi.credentials.admin` are given **only by a super administrator** (SQL `access_owner_only()`, app `OWNER_ONLY_CAPABILITIES`). The other high-risk capabilities (`it.remote.view`, `it.diagnostics`, the other high-risk Wi-Fi ones, `admin.*`) follow rule 4.
6. **A reason is required** for every high-risk capability and for `cctv.export` (at least three characters, kept on the grant), and for every removal (kept as `revoke_reason`). This applies to super administrators too.
7. Administration capabilities (`admin.*`) go only to people with the `admin` or `platform_owner` role (for anybody else they would do nothing on the screens but `admin.users` / `admin.access` would open every profile and grant through row security).
8. Revoking is allowed for any live grant of a person the administrator may manage (it only takes access away); the reason is required.
9. **Downloads only for administrators (owner ruling E-1).** `cctv.export` goes only to people with the `admin` or `platform_owner` role ("Download rights (export) can only be given to administrators and super administrators."), in the Server Action and in SQL like rule 7; the editor does not offer it for other roles and says why; presets for viewers and operators contain no export. Migration `20261021000017` revoked any live export grant of a non-administrator (none existed locally) with an audit row.
10. **Keeping a review item is giving it** (review M-10): `access_review_keep` applies rules 1-9 to the grant being confirmed.
11. **Role changes (review I-2)** go through `change_person_role(org, actor, user, role, expected_version)` in one transaction: leaving `admin` / `platform_owner` revokes every live `admin.*` and `cctv.export` grant ("Role changed: administration rights removed" / "Role changed: download rights removed"); a super administrator becoming anything else also loses the owner-only rights of rule 5 ("Role changed: rights only a super administrator may hold were removed"). Everything else stays; the people screen then shows "Review remaining access". The revocations are audited (`access_revoked`, with from/to role). Status changes go through `set_person_active` so the database knows who acted. Both check: the actor holds `admin.users` with an administrative role, is not the person, and may give the old and new role (as `canManageRole`).
12. The target person's row is locked before their role is read (review M-12), so a concurrent role change cannot slip between the check and the write.

## Super administrator (ruling P-1; migration `20261021000016`)

The `platform_owner` role is shown as **"Super administrator"** everywhere (stored value unchanged). Every active super administrator holds **every catalogue capability for the whole organisation** as explicit, revocable grants (source `manual`, reason "Platform owner: all capabilities"), kept so by: `grant_platform_owner_all(org, user)` (service role; the people screen calls it when it creates a super administrator); a trigger on `capabilities` (a capability added later is granted at once to every active super administrator of every organisation); a trigger on `profiles` (role becomes `platform_owner`, or a super administrator is reactivated → the full set, so a right removed meanwhile comes back). The access editor shows "Super administrator: full rights (all capabilities)", lists what is missing and offers **Restore full rights** (and the preset "Full rights (all capabilities)" in the Administration area), given by another super administrator. Demoting keeps the grants (a role change never changes grants); remove them in the editor. Super administrators are exempt from rule 4 ("hold it yourself") and rule 5, **not** from MFA (D-10, once enforced in P3), fresh authentication, audit, the reason of rule 6, or rule 2.

Fix round 1 additions: new grants carry the reason "Super administrator: all capabilities" (older rows keep "Platform owner: all capabilities" — history is immutable — and the screens show them with the new words); every automatic grant (promotion, reactivation, new or newly organisation-scoped catalogue capability) writes `access_granted` with `source: platform_owner_auto`, the count, the grant ids and the acting person when known. **At least one active super administrator per organisation** is enforced in the database (trigger `profiles_keep_super_admin`, SQLSTATE `DR001`: deactivating, demoting or deleting the last one is refused, also for the service role and for deleting the sign-in, which deletes the profile; serialised by locking the organisation row, so two super administrators acting on each other at the same moment cannot both succeed); the people screen shows "There must always be at least one active super administrator.". Only a super administrator creates, promotes, demotes, deactivates or resets another super administrator, and an administrator can neither revoke nor narrow a super administrator's grants. Reactivating a super administrator offers no "remove all": the full set comes back by itself. Test and demo clean-up uses `access_teardown_test_org_people` (refuses every organisation but `test-…` and the demo).

**Single super administrator and the review (review M-11).** Nobody confirms their own access (rule 2), so the migrated grants of the only super administrator could never be confirmed. Resolution: a second super administrator reviews them; or, while there is exactly one active super administrator, a **break-glass self-confirmation** on the review page: a typed reason of at least 10 characters, recorded as `self_confirmed` ("self-confirmed (single super administrator)") in `access_grant_reviews` and audited (`access_review_self_confirmed`). Refused (`DR002`) as soon as another active super administrator exists.

## The access editor (P2-3, `/admin/users/<id>/access`)

A change is a list of explicit grants to add and grants to remove; nothing is edited in place and history is never changed.

- **Current access**: every grant that is not removed — in effect, not started yet, ended (but not revoked) — grouped CCTV / IT / Wi-Fi / Administration, with where, valid from / until (IST, end of day), an **Expires soon** badge (ends within 7 days), where it came from (`manual`, `migrated:*`), the reason, the review flag and a **Remove…** button (reason required). **History** lists removed grants with who removed them, when and why.
- **Add access** — the directive's assignment cases as plain choices that expand into explicit grants: *All cameras in these locations* (location scope, includes cameras added later), *Selected cameras* (camera scope, across locations, per-location "Select all cameras of …" which creates camera grants; "new cameras are NOT included automatically"), *Whole organisation*; then a **coverage × right matrix** (rows: the organisation, each whole location, each location's chosen cameras; columns Live / Playback / Export) so live, playback and export are given independently (e.g. live for a location and export for two cameras). IT and Wi-Fi: rights per capability for the organisation, locations or selected assets. Administration: organisation-wide only. **Presets** fill the matrix or the list and are never stored: *Location CCTV viewer* (live + playback), *Location IT viewer* (`it.inventory.view` + `it.monitoring.view`), *Administrator set* (exactly the thirteen capabilities `migrated:admin` gave existing administrators: `cctv.live`, `cctv.playback`, `cctv.export`, `it.inventory.view`, `it.monitoring.view`, `it.monitoring.operate`, `wifi.inventory.view`, `wifi.config.view_redacted`, `admin.users`, `admin.access`, `admin.inventory`, `admin.alerts`, `admin.audit.read`; offered for administrators and super administrators; a unit test compares it with the migration, the dev-admin script and the demo), *Full rights* (super administrators). Only the rights the acting administrator may give are offered.
- **Valid until**: a day in India; the grant ends at midnight IST after it (`valid_until` = next day 00:00 IST, exclusive). A past day is refused; empty = no end.
- **Check the effect** calls `preview_access_change` (per right: gained and lost cameras / assets / locations with examples and their location; the exact grants to be created — "already given: nothing changes" for a grant an existing one covers — and removed). **Save these changes** is possible only for exactly the changes last checked: the save sends the preview's token (review M-5: an HMAC-SHA256 of person, access version and changes with the server secret `ACCESS_PREVIEW_SECRET`, purpose `access_preview`, minimum 16 characters; when it is not configured, a random key of the server process is used, so a token cannot be made outside the server and simply has to be checked again after a restart) and its `expected_version`; `apply_access_change` saves revocations and grants in one transaction. When the person's access changed meanwhile (another administrator, a script): "Someone else changed this person’s access while you were working. Reload the page…". Rate limits per administrator: preview 30/min, save 10/min, review 30/min.
- **Audit** (`admin_audit_log`): `access_granted` (grant ids, capability, scope kind and id, valid until, reason; count) and `access_revoked` (the same plus the removal reason) per save, in parts of at most 200 grants (`part` / `parts`; review M-4: no id is left out, however large the save); `access_review_kept` (grant ids, count, note); `access_review_self_confirmed`. No secrets.
- The interim editor of P2-1 (`setUserAccess`, `getUserAccess`, `saveAccessAction`, zones) is gone; `replace_user_grants` stays for tests and tooling only.

**Access review** (`/admin/access-review`, admin role AND `admin.access`): live grants with source `migrated:home`, `migrated:manager`, `migrated:admin` or the `needs_review` flag, per person, with **Keep (confirm)** (writes `access_grant_reviews`, append-only, once per grant, and an audit event), **Remove…** and **Convert to scoped grants** (open the editor with the removal prepared; Convert also opens "Add access" for the same right — locations for an organisation-wide grant, single cameras for a location grant); and the `access_migration_report` notes, people who deliberately lost the view of IT devices first. The count of open items is shown on the Users page (the administration home for access).

**People list**: role labels Viewer / Operator / Manager / Administrator / Super administrator with the sentence that a role gives no access by itself; the column "Access in effect now" ("Live: 3 locations, Playback: 3 locations, IT: none", from `access_grant_summary(org)`); a new person is created with no grants and the form offers "Assign access next"; "Change role…" says what the change removes (rule 11) and afterwards lists the remaining access ("Review remaining access") for administrators with `admin.access`. The reactivation list (review M-1) is shown only to an administrator who may manage the person, and — without `admin.access` — only while the person is deactivated. "Access review" is in the Admin navigation for `admin.access` holders. Deactivation text (P2-4): "They can no longer sign in, the sign-ins they have now are ended, and their open video stops within a minute. Their access is kept and applies again if they are reactivated." **Reactivating** first lists the person's live grants and offers "Reactivate and keep this access" or "Remove all of it and reactivate" (with a reason; needs `admin.access`; revokes exactly the listed grants at the listed access version, then activates).
